How breathing actually works

Chest expansion & why your heart rate follows your breath

Chest vs. stomach

Effective breathwork expands your chest - not your belly. When your ribcage opens outward, your lungs fill completely. Belly-breathing is shallow and doesn't activate the same calming response.

Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA)
♡

Your heart rate isn't constant - it naturally speeds up when you inhale and slows down when you exhale. This is RSA, and it's a sign of a healthy, responsive nervous system.

Slow, rhythmic breathing - like the pacer guides you through - amplifies RSA. Over time this trains your nervous system to shift out of anxiety faster.

Where to start

Begin with equal inhale and exhale - no holding. The goal is to gradually slow your breath until you reach about 3 breaths per minute. Only then do you add holds.

Once 10s in / 10s out feels comfortable, you can begin adding a short hold after the inhale - not before. This is the beginning of more advanced pranayama practice.

This progression follows traditional pranayama instruction - beginning with slow, equal breathing to steady the nervous system before introducing breath retention.
